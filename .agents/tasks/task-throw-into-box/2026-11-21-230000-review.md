# Sim "throw into box" policy (CEM), eval, and gated real-arm runner

Adds a MuJoCo gym env (ball + open box), a 27-parameter box-conditioned open-loop release trajectory trained with CEM, an eval script (85/100 in box on seeds 900000+), and `run_throw.py`, which defaults to a sim dry run and can drive the real arm through `Guard.move` behind `--real --i-have-a-human`. `core.py` is untouched. The problem is that the policy does not throw, and the success check can count a ball that is still in the gripper.

Watch for: (1) the trained behaviour is a placement/drop, not a throw (confirmed: ball released 0 to 3 cm from the box centre in xy at 6 to 9 cm height, peak ball speed about 1 m/s); (2) `in_box` terminates the episode at the first step the ball is inside the box volume, even before release (confirmed, seed 900007); (3) the real path has no pose or keep-out check, and the plan drives joints 2 and 3 to about 0.97 (confirmed); (4) six unrelated modified files are in the diff (confirmed).

**Verdict**: NEEDS_CHANGES

## High-level view

What the 85% measures is different from what the docs say. The learned motion carries the ball above the box and opens the gripper, so the number is for placement, and docs/11 still describes the task as a "short toss/lob". Success detection also credits a ball that is still held over the box. The headline number and the task description both need correcting before anyone relies on them.

The real-arm path lacks any check that the arm starts near the assumed start pose or that the commanded poses are safe. The plan was tuned in a simulator and pushes joints to near their limits.

The diff also bundles unrelated work (UI MCP server, `norma up/down/check`, mujoco branch in `autorun.py`, `kin.py`, station message draining, vision ROI/blob filter). These are uncommitted working-tree changes that belong in separate commits.

<summary>Issues (9)</summary>

1. **Policy is a placement, not a throw (confirmed)**: rename the claim, or add a success condition that requires release at a minimum distance or speed from the box, then retrain. Update docs/11 and CHANGELOG wording.
2. **Success counted while ball is held (confirmed)**: `in_box` fires on ball position alone, so a ball carried into the box volume counts. Require the gripper to have opened (and the ball to be released) and optionally require the ball to settle in the box.
3. **First-contact termination (likely)**: the episode ends as soon as the ball is inside the box, so a bounce-out is never seen. Run on until the ball rests or the horizon ends.
4. **No start-pose check on the real arm (confirmed)**: `real_run` never verifies the arm is near `START_NORMS` before replaying the plan, whose frame 0 assumes it. Abort if any joint is more than about 0.1 from start.
5. **Plan drives joints near limits with no keep-out (confirmed)**: targets of about 0.97 for joint 2 and 0.9 or more for joint 3, with `forbidden_boxes` empty. Populate `forbidden_boxes` for the table, box and person side, or clamp targets, and validate the plan with a dry-run listing before moving.
6. **Real-arm timing mismatch (likely)**: the plan was trained at 20 Hz and is replayed at 10 Hz, so duration and release steps are twice as long in real time. The docs mention "slower", but there is no validation that the slowed trajectory still reaches the box region.
7. **Unhandled exceptions leave the arm holding the last goal (likely)**: only `KeyboardInterrupt` calls `guard.estop()`. Any other exception (including `EOFError` from `input()`) goes straight to `stop_backend`. Estop on any exception.
8. **Box position unchecked (possible)**: `--box` accepts values outside the trained range (x within 0.12, y 0.25 to 0.38). Reject or warn out of range.
9. **Unrelated changes bundled (confirmed)**: split `.mcp.json`, `norma`, `autorun.py`, `kin.py`, `station_backend.py` and `vision.py` out. The station message-draining change affects the real-arm state path and the vision filter changes cup detection for other users.

## What the 85% actually measures

The probe on eval seeds (900000 to 900011) shows the pattern. The ball is released at x, y within about 3 cm of the box centre and at z of 0.06 to 0.09 m, just over the 0.06 m rim, and then falls. Peak ball speed across the episode is 0.9 to 1.5 m/s, and the arm targets are nearly identical across seeds (joint 2 at 0.96 to 0.98, joint 3 at 0.77 to 0.98). CEM has found the easy solution: reach over the box and open the gripper. That is legitimate for a sim success metric, but it is not the toss the env docstring and docs/11 describe. Since the winning motion depends on reach and not on release speed, the docs' caveat about inflated jaw-impulse ball speeds understates the issue: the sim number says little about throwing at all.

Failures cluster at the far edge: box y of 0.37 failed in three of the twelve probed seeds (900002, 900006, 900010), all with long durations (8 to 11 steps) and release at step 21 to 23 with the ball still at the rim height. The reach limit, not the throw, is the bottleneck, which again points to placement.

## Success detection

`in_box` tests only ball xy within 5.5 cm of the box centre and z below 5.5 cm. It does not check that the gripper has opened. On seed 900007 the episode ended with success at step 16 although the release step was 18, so the ball was still gripped, or fell from the jaws, when it was counted. Because `step` terminates on `success`, a ball that bounces out is also never observed. Both bias the eval upward. The reward (+10 on success) trains CEM toward exactly this behaviour.

## Eval and held-out seeds

Selection uses 10000 to 10011, the dataset uses 50000+, eval uses 900000+, and the training seeds are random in [0, 2^30), so overlap is possible only by chance (about 0.1% per eval seed). Eval draws only the box position from the seed; ball mass, friction and servo parameters are fixed, so "held-out" means held-out box positions in a deterministic simulator. With n=100 the 95% interval is about 77 to 91%. The final policy was chosen between two candidates on 12 episodes, which is a small selection step but not a leak of eval seeds.

## Real-arm path

The gating is missing a pose check. Frame 0 of the plan is START moved only 1/duration toward the target, and the Guard step limit (0.35) bounds each call relative to the current state, not to the plan. If the arm is anywhere other than the start pose (for instance left from a previous run), the first commands sweep it toward targets from an unexpected configuration, with joint 1 unconstrained between about 0.3 and 0.6 across the plan. `Guard.cfg.forbidden_boxes` is never populated, although docs/11 lists a keep-out zone as a requirement of any throw mode. The gripper release when `--with-ball` is set happens at a step index with no check that a ball is in the gripper or that the box is in place.

Exception handling covers only `KeyboardInterrupt`. A software fault mid-plan leaves the last goal active; the human at the power plug is the only mitigation.

## Unrelated changes in the diff

`.mcp.json` (new `elrobot-ui` server), `norma` (`up`, `down`, `check`), `scripts/autorun.py` (new mujoco backend and changed defaults: backend now defaults to `mujoco`, vision to `dark`, object to `black cup`), `src/normakit/kin.py` (+128 lines), `station_backend.py` (message draining in three listener loops, camera discovery helpers) and `vision.py` (ROI and blob filter) are uncommitted modifications unrelated to throwing. The autorun default change is a behaviour change for existing callers. The draining in `station_backend.py` drops all but the newest message per queue, which is correct only if every consumer needs just the latest state, and that was not verified in this review (possible).

Also, the throw scene includes `elrobot_scene.xml` and `hulls/`, which are untracked in git, so the env and eval cannot be reproduced from a clean checkout. docs/11 acknowledges this for the A100 host only.

## Test coverage

Not tested: the real path (never run), `run_throw.py` argument gating (no test that `--real` without the human flag refuses), success detection against a ball held in the gripper, and plan clipping against `Guard` limits.
