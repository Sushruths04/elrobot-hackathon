SYSTEM

How it works

  • Look first: a fresh photo right before every move; positions are measured live, never hard-coded.
  • Move safely: IK path checks, slow near objects, current watchdog; nothing moves unless the human pressed ARM.
  • Record everything: every task is filmed and saved as a training episode, trimmed to the task.
PART 1

The task we worked towards

A person shows it once, by hand:

  1. open the flip-top cap of a tissue pack
  2. pinch one tissue and pull it out
  3. clear the coffee beans next to it

Before that: warm-up tasks (marker into a box, a magnetic duster collecting metal caps) to get calibration, grasping and recording right.

PART 2

Warm-up: marker into the white box

The first autonomous run pushed the box: the table mapping had x mirrored. Found from the video, fixed with a calibration that uses the jaw tip itself. Afterwards: first-try holds, the gripper stall reading tells marker (1550-1590 ticks) from thin pen (1397) from nothing (1322).

PART 3

Magnetic duster collects the metal caps

Hold the duster ~1.2 cm above each cap for 1 s — no pressing, the magnet takes the cap. A camera check counts the caps left before going to the box. The failure: the detector saw only one of two caps.

PART 4

Opening the flip-top cap

  1. Push down: failed — and the vision model said "open" while the arm hid a closed lid.
  2. Hand-guided demo (soft hold, no leader arm): a sideways lift opens it.
  3. Replay of that demo: lid semi-open (pack 2 cm off).
  4. Autonomous sideways sweep at one height: cap open on attempt 3.
PART 5

Pinch and pull one tissue

Too deep and the gripper lifts the whole pack (the script even printed "tissue pulled"). Pinch at the tissue's tip, ~8-9 cm, close fully, pull straight up. A thin sheet does not stall the gripper (1324-1334 ticks either way), so only the camera can confirm the hold.

PART 6

Clearing the coffee beans

Wiping with the held tissue spread the beans. Lanes at table level cleared the bean area but scattered the beans. Pushing the whole pack through them in one stroke was the user's favourite — saved as a demo.

PART 7

The whole task in one run

Guided by short chat commands, and then autonomous with a camera check after every stage: cap open (picture change), tissue held (camera), retry if not. The bean stage still needs work.

PART 8

Wooden spoon scoops coffee beans into the cup

24 LeRobot v3 teleop episodes recorded at real 30.0 fps (table + wrist cameras, 0 stale frames) after fixing USB hub drops and leader j4 range. Autonomous shifted demo-matching grasped the spork under load (1355 ticks, 97 mA) and carried beans across to the cup.

PART 9

Simulation twin & RL policies

Calibrated MuJoCo 3.2.7 twin: 94/100 autonomous cup pick & 3 s hold with wrist-camera 3D alignment, plus 30 Hz PPO ball balancing (93.3% front-camera) matched 1:1 to the real sideways JOOLA bat grip.

LESSONS

What we learned

  • Measure the object right before moving. Ten pinches missed because the pack had moved 10 cm since the last photo.
  • Never trust a yes/no for physical success. The model said "lid open" and "tissue held" when they were not; the script printed "tissue pulled" while holding the whole pack.
  • Show, then repeat. A 30-second hand-guided demo beat hours of tuning a push; replays work if the object is within 1-2 cm.
  • Hold gently, never press. Pressing down pushed the pack; the duster magnet works from a distance; slow, soft accelerations keep things in the gripper.
  • One clean move beats many small steps. Go to the working height in one move, then sweep at that height.
  • Film and log everything. The video found the mirrored calibration and the false "lid open".
OUTLOOK

Next

  • Train a policy on the recorded LeRobot episodes (successes only; failures kept as labelled negatives).
  • Grippy fingertip pads for flat tissues; a top-down pinch for the tip.
  • Bean collection: push to a corner instead of spreading; verify with a before/after photo.
  • Compensate the sag at long reach (the tip drops about 8 mm at low stretched poses).
